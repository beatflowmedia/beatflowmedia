# Draft: subscription sync licence

**Status: PUBLISHED AS `sync-license@2026-10-16` and in force. Not reviewed by a lawyer.**

**Written for:** a lawyer reviewing BeatFlow Media Group's subscription licence. It assumes no familiarity with the platform, so the behaviour the clauses describe is set out in full.

This went live rather than sitting in draft because the paid tiers have advertised *"commercial use"* and *"YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, podcasts"* since before any text existed to back them. No one had subscribed, so the gap was latent rather than breached — publishing something accurate closes it before the first customer rather than after.

---

## What is being sold

Four monthly tiers: Student $9.99, Creator $24, Professional $49, Agency $149. Each grants the right to synchronise BFMG recordings with video and publish the result, including monetised content, for as long as the subscription is active.

## The clause that matters most

> Anything you publish while subscribed stays licensed permanently. What ends with the subscription is the right to publish NEW content using our music.

This is the industry norm — Epidemic Sound and Artlist both operate it — and it is not optional in practice. A creator with three years of videos cannot have them become infringing because they cancelled a $24 plan. They would not subscribe, and they would be right not to.

**It creates a drafting problem worth naming:** the grant is time-bound, but the clearance of any individual published work is perpetual. Those are two different durations in one licence. The platform records which, and when, via the certificate.

Epidemic and Artlist solve the evidential side with channel "safelisting" or a "Clearlist" — the creator registers each channel or video while subscribed. BFMG does not require that, because every licence already carries a dated, verifiable record at `/verify/<id>`. Worth a reviewer's view on whether that record is sufficient evidence of *when* something was published, given it proves when the licence was held, not when the video went up.

## What the platform can actually deliver

| Claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| We will not claim or mute a subscriber's video | BFMG has never purchased DistroKid's Social Media Pack for any release, so no recording is enrolled in YouTube Content ID or Meta Rights Manager. Enrolment is opt-in and paid. |
| Worldwide, non-exclusive | BFMG controls the masters; nothing is licensed exclusively to anyone. |
| Monetised content permitted | Nothing prevents it once the above holds. |

**Stated as a risk rather than hidden:** AI-generated music can occasionally trigger a false match from an unrelated rights holder whose catalogue it resembles. Outside BFMG's control. The terms say "we do not claim" — not "no claim can occur."

## Exclusions

Broadcast television and radio, cinema, paid advertising campaigns, video games, resale of the music on its own, and AI training. Each needs a separate negotiated licence.

## Claim support

> Our catalogue is not registered with YouTube Content ID or Meta Rights Manager, so we do not claim or mute our subscribers' videos. If a third party claims one anyway, your account provides a dispute pack — your licence number, its verification link, and a statement you can submit to the platform. We log every claim.

**Deliberately not promised: that BFMG will get a claim cleared.** Platforms accept disputes only from the account that uploaded the video, so BFMG cannot submit one, and cannot compel a third party to release a claim it made in error. Offering to "resolve" claims would be a promise that depends on someone else's cooperation.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. **The two durations.** Is "the grant is time-bound, the clearance of published work is perpetual" expressed clearly enough to survive a dispute with a cancelled subscriber?

2. **Evidence of publication date.** The licence record proves what was held and when. It does not prove when a video was published. Does the clause need the subscriber to retain their own evidence of publication?

3. **AI provenance.** The recordings are AI-assisted or AI-generated. The existing Terms already say copyright in AI material is unsettled and that rights here are contractual. Does the sync licence need its own version of that, given a sync licensee's exposure is larger than a listener's?

4. **Tier differences.** Student, Creator, Professional and Agency advertise different rights — client work, broadcast, white-label. The published text does not yet distinguish them. Either the text gains per-tier scope or the marketing claims narrow.

5. **Statutory cancellation rights.** CJEU C-234/25 (2026) treats streaming subscriptions as digital *services*, so the art.16(m) exclusion captured for downloads does not reach them. An EU or UK subscriber retains a 14-day cancellation right. The Terms say so; does the sync licence need its own statement?

6. **Jurisdiction.** The Terms specify New Jersey law and AAA arbitration. A sync licensee may be anywhere. Is that enforceable against an international subscriber?

## Not legal advice

Drafted to describe the platform's real behaviour accurately, which is the part that can be verified. Whether any of it is enforceable has not been assessed by anyone qualified.
